# EMOPET — France Veterinary Prescriber Channel Strategy

**Date:** 2026-09-06  
**Status:** STRATEGIC WORKING DOCUMENT — NOT LEGAL ADVICE / NOT A PRODUCT VALIDATION CLAIM  
**Scope:** French veterinary channel, prescriber strategy, distribution sequencing, channel economics and guardrails.

## 1. Executive decision

EMOPET should **not** use the French veterinary channel as its primary volume-distribution channel at launch.

The recommended commercial architecture is:

1. **D2C as the primary volume and margin engine**;
2. **veterinarians as prescribers, credibility partners and early-adopter users**;
3. **scientific institutions and VetTech networks as proof/credibility infrastructure**;
4. **groups, purchasing structures and independent multicard representatives only after product evidence and incorporation**;
5. **no diagnostic positioning and no sales mechanism that could compromise veterinary independence**.

The strongest veterinary proposition is not “EMOPET detects disease”. It is:

> **EMOPET improves the longitudinal history available before and during the veterinary consultation. It does not make the diagnosis.**

Internal shorthand: **EMOPET enriches the anamnesis.**

## 2. Evidence-supported channel findings

### 2.1 Delegate channel

There is no reliable public national count of independent veterinary delegates in France. The often-cited estimate of roughly 100–200 independents / 10% of the profession is therefore not to be treated as established evidence.

The dominant model is salaried commercial representation for veterinary laboratories, diagnostics and pet-food companies. Independent multicard representatives exist, but the segment is marginal and not publicly quantified.

**Implication:** do not build the launch plan around recruiting a veterinary delegate network.

### 2.2 Veterinary market structure

France has a dense veterinary network but purchasing and commercial decisions are increasingly consolidated through veterinary groups, central purchasing structures and networks.

This reduces the efficiency of a clinic-by-clinic sales strategy and increases the strategic value of:

- central group pilots;
- national or regional referencing;
- veterinary-owned purchasing/distribution structures;
- early-adopter networks capable of creating professional credibility before commercial scale.

### 2.3 Channel economics

The veterinary resale/distribution channel can create a heavy cumulative margin stack once clinic margin, distributor margin and potentially representative commission are combined.

For EMOPET, which combines hardware and recurring software/service economics, this makes veterinary retail structurally less attractive than D2C as the primary channel.

**Commercial doctrine:** veterinary recommendation can be highly valuable even when veterinary resale is not the main source of revenue.

### 2.4 Deontological boundary

Veterinary independence must be preserved. Any model involving commissions, incentives, resale, commercial links, professional access or research remuneration requires careful legal/deontological structuring.

Permanent internal guardrails:

- no hidden commission to a veterinarian for recommending EMOPET;
- no incentive tied to diagnosis, referral or clinical decision;
- no claim that EMOPET replaces veterinary judgment;
- no product output framed as a clinical conclusion;
- paid veterinary work must correspond to real, documented services such as methodology review, training, research or advisory work;
- conflict-of-interest and disclosure requirements must be reviewed before launch.

## 3. Prescriber value proposition

### 3.1 The problem to solve

A recurring consultation problem is poor longitudinal recall. Owners frequently arrive with statements such as:

- “he has been different lately”;
- “she seems less comfortable than before”;
- “I think this started a few weeks ago”;
- “he is not sleeping like he used to”.

The commercial opportunity for EMOPET is to improve the quality of that history without pretending to identify the clinical cause.

### 3.2 The one-sentence veterinary pitch

> **Your clients often know that something has changed, but not exactly when. EMOPET helps reconstruct when the change began and how it differs from that dog’s own usual pattern.**

Alternative compact formulation:

> **We do not tell you what the dog has. We help you see what changed before the dog arrived in your consultation.**

### 3.3 What veterinarians should receive

The preferred prescriber-facing object is a **Veterinary Summary**, not a complex clinical dashboard.

The default summary should contain only longitudinal observations and provenance, for example:

- observation period;
- individual reference period;
- persistent changes observed;
- approximate first persistent appearance;
- frequency / recurrence;
- confidence level;
- relevant context;
- periods in which EMOPET abstained or had insufficient information;
- explicit statement that no clinical cause has been inferred.

**Product principle:** observation first, interpretation by the veterinarian.

## 4. Four prescriber mechanisms to evaluate

### 4.1 Veterinary Summary

Owner-generated/shareable one-page synthesis for consultation.

Purpose:

- improve chronology;
- reduce reconstruction from memory;
- provide structured observations;
- preserve veterinary interpretation.

This is the highest-priority veterinary-channel product mechanism.

### 4.2 Free practitioner access

Potential later option: a free professional view for animals whose owners explicitly choose to share information with a veterinarian.

Design requirements before implementation:

- owner-controlled sharing;
- revocable access;
- access logging;
- strict separation between observation and diagnosis;
- no compulsory account/workflow burden if a one-page report is sufficient;
- data-protection/legal review before release.

The professional interface must reduce workload rather than create another dashboard to monitor.

### 4.3 Practitioner referral code with no veterinarian commission

Candidate acquisition mechanism:

- veterinarian gives a code to an owner;
- owner receives a defined EMOPET benefit;
- veterinarian receives **no sales commission**;
- EMOPET can measure attributable recommendation volume.

Strategic advantage: recommendation remains economically independent from the veterinarian.

**Status:** candidate model only. Obtain legal/deontological review before launch and do not describe it as formally compliant until reviewed.

### 4.4 Optional clinic resale

Some clinics may prefer to stock/resell EMOPET.

This should remain optional, not the core prescriber model.

Before activation, validate:

- permissibility of resale in the precise EMOPET context;
- clinical-commercial separation;
- pricing and clinic margin;
- inventory and returns;
- warranty/SAV responsibility;
- conflict-of-interest disclosures where applicable.

## 5. Commercial sequencing

### Phase 0 — pre-incorporation / pre-proof

**Goal:** credibility and product usefulness, not sales volume.

Priority actions:

- scientific review and validation planning;
- Oniris / veterinary-science discussions;
- ENVT / other qualified veterinary reviewers where relevant;
- Serpell / C-BARQ scientific governance;
- VetTech ecosystem participation;
- observe AFVAC and similar professional environments;
- design the Veterinary Summary with veterinarians before building a full professional portal.

**Do not hire a veterinary sales delegate at this stage.**

### Phase 1 — prototype / early veterinary proof

Recruit a small panel of veterinarians to test the **usefulness of the information**, not the clinical validity of diagnoses.

Suggested initial panel: approximately 10–20 practitioners, expanding to 20–50 only once the report format is stable.

Primary questions:

1. Did the summary help you understand the chronology faster?
2. Did it provide useful information that owner recall alone may have missed?
3. Did it save time or add time?
4. Was the separation between observation and interpretation clear?
5. Did any wording feel diagnostic or intrusive?
6. Which observations are actually useful in consultation?

### Phase 2 — D2C launch + prescriber network

D2C remains the main sales route.

Veterinary role:

- recommendation;
- early-adopter credibility;
- feedback;
- professional education;
- optional referral code without veterinarian commission, if legally cleared;
- owner-controlled report sharing.

Commercial KPIs should distinguish:

- veterinary recommendation rate;
- conversion from veterinarian-attributed referrals;
- Veterinary Summary usage;
- veterinarian usefulness score;
- time saved / time added;
- D2C CAC by channel;
- subscription retention;
- complaint / false-alert / over-interpretation rates.

### Phase 3 — structured veterinary distribution experiments

Only after product evidence and incorporation:

- test one independent multicard representative on a bounded territory;
- approach veterinary groups for structured pilots;
- evaluate selected purchasing/distribution structures;
- assess clinic resale only where economics make sense;
- approach insurers only under strict data-governance constraints.

Any independent representative mandate must receive legal review before signature, especially if the relationship could qualify as an agent-commercial arrangement with statutory termination consequences.

## 6. Negotiation map

### Veterinary groups

Potential discussion topics:

- limited pilot scope;
- number of clinics;
- pre-agreed success criteria;
- staff training;
- Veterinary Summary workflow;
- pricing;
- optional resale economics;
- data controller/processor roles;
- publication / pilot evidence;
- co-marketing rights;
- name/logo usage;
- exclusivity.

**Exclusivity rule:** do not grant broad exclusivity without material reciprocal value, defined scope, duration and performance commitments.

### Purchasing/distribution structures

Potential terms:

- catalogue/reference conditions;
- distributor margin;
- minimum order / stock;
- payment terms;
- returns;
- logistics;
- after-sales support;
- training material;
- territory and exclusivity.

### Independent multicard representative

Potential terms:

- territory;
- target accounts;
- prohibited competing portfolio;
- remuneration;
- expenses;
- technical-training obligations;
- CRM/reporting;
- duration;
- termination;
- ownership of commercial data and leads.

No representative agreement should be signed before qualified review of the legal status and termination economics.

## 7. Insurer channel — opportunity with a hard data wall

Animal-health insurers may be commercially interesting because they already have a direct owner relationship and an economic interest in prevention and retention.

Possible commercial models to explore later:

- subsidised hardware;
- member benefit;
- subscription contribution;
- co-branded prevention programme;
- genuinely anonymised/aggregated research or population-level insights where lawful and appropriate.

**Internal strategic guardrail:** insurer access to individual MAT/TAG/ELI behavioural or wellbeing data must not be assumed. In particular, EMOPET should not casually enable individual data to be used for underwriting, pricing, eligibility or claims decisions.

Any insurer-data model requires a dedicated legal, ethical and product-governance decision.

## 8. Claim language for the veterinary channel

Preferred vocabulary:

- longitudinal observation;
- individual reference / baseline;
- chronology;
- persistent change;
- contextual observation;
- level of confidence;
- information insufficient;
- owner-shared history;
- veterinary interpretation remains necessary.

Avoid without specific validation/legal basis:

- diagnosis;
- disease detection;
- pain detection;
- arthritis detection;
- clinical alert;
- medical monitoring;
- veterinary decision support;
- early diagnosis.

Even when a condition such as osteoarthritis is discussed internally as an example of why longitudinal observation may matter, EMOPET must not claim that MAT/TAG identifies that condition unless a separate validated and legally reviewed product claim is deliberately pursued.

## 9. Data-governance gate

The source research correctly identifies data sharing with veterinarians as a material governance issue. However, the precise lawful basis, controller/processor roles and contractual structure must be determined from the actual implementation.

**Do not encode the statement that “Article 32 GDPR requires explicit consent for every veterinary transfer” as legal doctrine.** Article 32 primarily concerns security of processing. EMOPET may still deliberately choose explicit owner-controlled sharing as a product/ethics rule, but legal basis and role allocation require qualified review.

Required before professional sharing goes live:

- data-flow map;
- controller/processor determination;
- lawful-basis assessment;
- owner-facing permission UX;
- revocation mechanism;
- access logging;
- retention rules;
- contractual framework where required;
- security review.

## 10. Go / no-go commercial doctrine

### GO

- D2C core launch;
- veterinarian recommendation;
- one-page Veterinary Summary;
- scientific/VetTech credibility strategy;
- small early-adopter practitioner panel;
- measured referral attribution without veterinarian commission, subject to review;
- later bounded group/distributor experiments.

### NOT YET

- nationwide delegate network;
- broad clinic stocking programme;
- exclusivity with a veterinary group;
- insurer access to individual EMOPET data;
- clinical/dashboard positioning;
- disease-specific marketing claims.

### NO-GO WITHOUT A DELIBERATE PRODUCT RECLASSIFICATION / VALIDATION PROGRAMME

- presenting EMOPET as diagnosing a disease;
- claiming that a MAT/TAG pattern establishes pain, osteoarthritis, cardiac disease, anxiety disorder or another clinical condition;
- paying veterinarians in a manner tied to clinical decisions or hidden referral commissions;
- presenting scientific partners as endorsing EMOPET when they have not done so.

## 11. Evidence status and caveats

This document is a strategic synthesis derived from the 2026-09-06 France veterinary-delegate/channel research and current EMOPET doctrine.

Important caveats retained from the source research:

- no official published count of French veterinary delegates / independents was identified;
- market consolidation figures can move quickly;
- distributor/clinic margin ranges are indicative and require real commercial quotations;
- current connected-device benchmarks do not prove EMOPET channel viability;
- group, resale, referral-code, representative and data-sharing structures require qualified legal/deontological review before execution.
