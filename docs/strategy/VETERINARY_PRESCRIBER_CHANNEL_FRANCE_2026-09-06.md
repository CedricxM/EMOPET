# EMOPET — France Veterinary Prescriber Channel Strategy

**Date:** 2026-09-06  
**Status:** STRATEGIC WORKING DOCUMENT — NOT LEGAL ADVICE / NOT A PRODUCT VALIDATION CLAIM  
**Scope:** French veterinary channel, prescriber strategy, distribution sequencing, channel economics, prescriber acquisition and guardrails.

## 1. Executive decision

EMOPET should **not** use the French veterinary channel as its primary volume-distribution channel at launch.

The recommended commercial architecture is:

1. **D2C as the primary volume and margin engine**;
2. **veterinarians as prescribers, credibility partners and early-adopter users**;
3. **scientific institutions and VetTech networks as proof/credibility infrastructure**;
4. **groups, purchasing structures and independent multicard representatives only after product evidence and incorporation**;
5. **no diagnostic positioning and no sales mechanism that could compromise veterinary independence**.

The strongest veterinary proposition is not “EMOPET detects disease”. It is:

> **EMOPET improves the longitudinal history available before and during the veterinary consultation. It does not make the diagnosis.**

Internal shorthand: **EMOPET enriches the anamnesis.**

## 2. Evidence-supported channel findings

### 2.1 Delegate channel

There is no reliable public national count of independent veterinary delegates in France. The often-cited estimate of roughly 100–200 independents / 10% of the profession is therefore not to be treated as established evidence.

The dominant model is salaried commercial representation for veterinary laboratories, diagnostics and pet-food companies. Independent multicard representatives exist, but the segment is marginal and not publicly quantified.

**Implication:** do not build the launch plan around recruiting a veterinary delegate network.

### 2.2 Veterinary market structure

France has a dense veterinary network but purchasing and commercial decisions are increasingly consolidated through veterinary groups, central purchasing structures and networks.

This reduces the efficiency of a clinic-by-clinic sales strategy and increases the strategic value of:

- central group pilots;
- national or regional referencing;
- veterinary-owned purchasing/distribution structures;
- early-adopter networks capable of creating professional credibility before commercial scale.

### 2.3 Channel economics

The veterinary resale/distribution channel can create a heavy cumulative margin stack once clinic margin, distributor margin and potentially representative commission are combined.

For EMOPET, which combines hardware and recurring software/service economics, this makes veterinary retail structurally less attractive than D2C as the primary channel.

**Commercial doctrine:** veterinary recommendation can be highly valuable even when veterinary resale is not the main source of revenue.

### 2.4 Deontological boundary

Veterinary independence must be preserved. Any model involving commissions, incentives, resale, commercial links, professional access or research remuneration requires careful legal/deontological structuring.

Permanent internal guardrails:

- no hidden commission to a veterinarian for recommending EMOPET;
- no incentive tied to diagnosis, referral or clinical decision;
- no claim that EMOPET replaces veterinary judgment;
- no product output framed as a clinical conclusion;
- paid veterinary work must correspond to real, documented services such as methodology review, training, research or advisory work;
- conflict-of-interest and disclosure requirements must be reviewed before launch.

## 3. Prescriber value proposition

### 3.1 The problem to solve

A recurring consultation problem is poor longitudinal recall. Owners frequently arrive with statements such as:

- “he has been different lately”;
- “she seems less comfortable than before”;
- “I think this started a few weeks ago”;
- “he is not sleeping like he used to”.

The commercial opportunity for EMOPET is to improve the quality of that history without pretending to identify the clinical cause.

### 3.2 The one-sentence veterinary pitch

> **Your clients often know that something has changed, but not exactly when. EMOPET helps reconstruct when the change began and how it differs from that dog’s own usual pattern.**

Alternative compact formulation:

> **We do not tell you what the dog has. We help you see what changed before the dog arrived in your consultation.**

### 3.3 What veterinarians should receive

The preferred prescriber-facing object is a **Veterinary Summary**, not a complex clinical dashboard.

The default summary should contain only longitudinal observations and provenance, for example:

- observation period;
- individual reference period;
- persistent changes observed;
- approximate first persistent appearance;
- frequency / recurrence;
- confidence level;
- relevant context;
- periods in which EMOPET abstained or had insufficient information;
- explicit statement that no clinical cause has been inferred.

**Product principle:** observation first, interpretation by the veterinarian.

## 4. Four prescriber mechanisms to evaluate

### 4.1 Veterinary Summary

Owner-generated/shareable one-page synthesis for consultation.

Purpose:

- improve chronology;
- reduce reconstruction from memory;
- provide structured observations;
- preserve veterinary interpretation.

This is the highest-priority veterinary-channel product mechanism.

### 4.2 Free practitioner access

Potential later option: a free professional view for animals whose owners explicitly choose to share information with a veterinarian.

Design requirements before implementation:

- owner-controlled sharing;
- revocable access;
- access logging;
- strict separation between observation and diagnosis;
- no compulsory account/workflow burden if a one-page report is sufficient;
- data-protection/legal review before release.

The professional interface must reduce workload rather than create another dashboard to monitor.

### 4.3 Practitioner referral code with no veterinarian commission

Candidate acquisition mechanism:

- veterinarian gives a code or QR access to an owner;
- owner receives a defined EMOPET benefit;
- veterinarian receives **no sales commission**;
- EMOPET can measure attributable recommendation volume.

Strategic advantage: recommendation remains economically independent from the veterinarian.

**Status:** candidate model only. Obtain legal/deontological review before launch and do not describe it as formally compliant until reviewed.

### 4.4 Optional clinic resale

Some clinics may prefer to stock/resell EMOPET.

This should remain optional, not the core prescriber model.

Before activation, validate:

- permissibility of resale in the precise EMOPET context;
- clinical-commercial separation;
- pricing and clinic margin;
- inventory and returns;
- warranty/SAV responsibility;
- conflict-of-interest disclosures where applicable.

## 5. Owner benefits for veterinarian-referred customers

A veterinarian referral should create **additional owner value**, not merely a discount code.

Candidate package for owners arriving through a veterinary prescriber:

- extended premium trial or several subscription months included;
- Veterinary Summary enabled from the start;
- guided onboarding during the first weeks;
- one-click pre-consultation export/share flow;
- simplified owner-controlled sharing with the referring clinic;
- potentially extended trial, warranty or support conditions;
- explicit statement that no data are shared automatically with the veterinarian.

Internal principle:

> **The veterinarian should be able to give the client a better EMOPET experience without becoming a reseller, support desk or paid affiliate.**

Suggested framing: **EMOPET Observation Pass** or equivalent, rather than a consumer-style promo code.

## 6. Pre-consultation product loop

The highest-value veterinary workflow may be event-driven rather than dashboard-driven.

When an owner has a veterinary appointment, EMOPET should eventually be able to offer:

> **Prepare my veterinary summary**

The owner then selects a period such as 30 / 60 / 90 days and can:

- review the summary;
- share it directly if authorised;
- download/print it;
- revoke access later where applicable.

This avoids requiring veterinarians to monitor another live interface.

**Design target:** a practitioner should understand the essential content in approximately 20 seconds.

## 7. Target veterinary personas

Do not target all veterinary clinics equally.

Initial high-value profiles:

1. **general companion-animal practitioners with long-term client relationships**;
2. **practitioners with a high proportion of ageing/senior dogs**;
3. **clinicians interested in internal medicine, pain, mobility or behaviour**, where longitudinal owner history is often important;
4. **digitally curious / VetTech-friendly practitioners**;
5. **veterinarians who repeatedly follow the same dogs across months or years**.

Lower initial priority:

- purely episodic/emergency-only environments where longitudinal follow-up is limited;
- clinics with low appetite for owner-generated digital information;
- channels primarily interested in resale margin rather than information utility.

The core selection question is:

> **Which practitioner immediately understands the value of knowing what changed, when, and relative to that dog’s own reference?**

## 8. Veterinary Founding Panel

### 8.1 Purpose

Before recruiting a sales force, EMOPET should recruit **10–15 founding veterinary practitioners** to help shape the veterinary-facing experience.

Working name: **EMOPET Veterinary Founding Panel**.

This is not a sales programme and does not imply endorsement.

### 8.2 Suggested format

Duration: approximately 3–6 months.

Low-burden participation:

- 2–3 interviews of roughly 30 minutes;
- critique of Veterinary Summary prototypes;
- optional testing with synthetic or later pilot data under an appropriate framework;
- structured feedback on wording, usefulness, workload and interpretation risk.

### 8.3 Questions to test

- What information would you actually read before/during a consultation?
- What is unnecessary?
- Which wording feels diagnostic or overconfident?
- Is 7, 30, 60 or 90 days of history most useful for different consultation contexts?
- Does the report save time or add work?
- Which observations help reconstruct chronology?
- What would make you recommend EMOPET to an owner?
- What would make you refuse to recommend it?
- Would you prefer a one-page report, a link, a portal, or a combination?
- What should never appear in an EMOPET veterinary-facing surface?

### 8.4 Value offered to panel members

Possible non-sales-linked benefits:

- early access to practitioner-facing prototypes;
- free future professional access where the product model supports it;
- direct feedback channel to EMOPET;
- acknowledgement as a founding advisory/pilot contributor only with explicit permission and accurate wording;
- invitations to later pilots or methodology discussions.

No member should be presented publicly as endorsing EMOPET without explicit written agreement.

## 9. How to reach the first veterinarians

### 9.1 Warm introductions first

Highest-priority acquisition source: **trusted introductions through the existing scientific and regional ecosystem**.

Potential introduction routes include:

- Oniris / relevant veterinary contacts;
- ENVT / relevant veterinary contacts;
- AudéLor / regional innovation network;
- later scientific partners and reviewers where appropriate;
- VetTech communities and professional events.

The ask is not “find customers for EMOPET”.

The preferred ask is:

> **Do you know one or two companion-animal practitioners who would be willing to criticise a one-page longitudinal summary designed for use before a consultation?**

This preserves the scientific/professional relationship and avoids turning academic contacts into sales intermediaries.

### 9.2 Geographic focus

Initial direct outreach should favour the Grand Ouest, where EMOPET can realistically meet practitioners and later run pilots:

**Lorient → Vannes → Nantes → Rennes**, with extension to nearby cities only after the first panel is populated.

The goal is not national coverage. The goal is a dense first learning network.

### 9.3 Targeted direct outreach

Build an initial target list of roughly **50 practices maximum**, scored on:

- companion-animal focus;
- long-term follow-up relationship;
- senior-dog caseload;
- relevant clinical interests;
- visible innovation/digital appetite;
- willingness to test owner-facing information tools;
- practical proximity for early pilots.

Do not mass-email hundreds of clinics at pre-prototype stage.

### 9.4 Independent veterinary networks

Independent-practice networks and groupings may later provide efficient access to practitioner panels without immediately negotiating national distribution.

Potential approach:

> **EMOPET is recruiting a small number of independent companion-animal practitioners to co-design and critique a longitudinal owner-to-vet summary. Could you introduce us to members interested in VetTech and consultation workflow?**

Examples identified during initial channel work include ClubVET and Vetoccitan. Current size, access conditions and willingness to circulate a panel request must be verified before external claims or outreach.

## 10. Outreach message doctrine

Do not lead with “AI”, “connected collar”, “innovative monitoring solution” or a sales pitch.

Lead with the consultation problem and the request for criticism.

Suggested first-contact structure:

> Bonjour Docteur,
>
> Je développe EMOPET, un projet français qui cherche à documenter les changements de routine d’un chien entre deux consultations, sans diagnostic ni interprétation clinique.
>
> Nous travaillons actuellement sur une synthèse d’une page destinée à répondre plus précisément à des questions très simples : qu’est-ce qui a changé, depuis quand, à quelle fréquence et avec quel niveau de confiance ?
>
> Avant de la construire davantage, je cherche un petit nombre de vétérinaires praticiens prêts à nous dire ce qui serait réellement utile ou inutile en consultation.
>
> Il ne s’agit ni d’une démarche de vente ni d’un engagement à utiliser EMOPET. Je souhaiterais simplement vous montrer une maquette pendant 20 à 30 minutes et recueillir vos critiques.
>
> Seriez-vous disponible pour un court échange ?

The objective is to earn **criticism and product insight**, not a purchase order.

## 11. Professional events as acquisition accelerators

Veterinary congresses and VetTech events should be treated as **high-density practitioner discovery environments**, not merely exhibition opportunities.

For AFVAC and comparable events, EMOPET should ideally arrive with:

- a working/simulated Veterinary Summary prototype;
- a 30-second explanation;
- a QR code for the Founding Panel / early-adopter interest list;
- a structured 3–5 question feedback form;
- no unsupported medical claims.

The single most useful live test question may be:

> **If a client brought you this in consultation, would you look at it?**

Follow-up:

- What would you remove?
- What is missing?
- What would make you trust the chronology?
- Would this save you time?

ASV / veterinary-nurse teams should not be ignored. They often shape owner communication, product explanations and clinic workflow. Their feedback should be collected separately from veterinarian feedback.

**External event dates, attendance figures and programme details must be re-verified before budgeting or publishing them.**

## 12. Prescriber acquisition funnel

Target funnel:

**scientific / regional introduction**  
→ **Founding Panel conversation**  
→ **Veterinary Summary critique**  
→ **early prototype/pilot participation**  
→ **prescriber onboarding**  
→ **clinic-specific QR / Observation Pass**  
→ **owner D2C purchase**  
→ **owner returns with useful summary**  
→ **veterinarian experiences the value directly**  
→ **repeat recommendation**

This creates a prescriber flywheel in which the strongest sales argument is the practitioner's own experience of the next consultation.

## 13. Commercial attribution model

The prescriber model must be measurable even without paying the veterinarian.

Each clinic/practitioner can eventually receive a unique referral identifier or QR.

Illustrative metrics:

- number of active prescribers;
- recommendations issued per active prescriber/month;
- referral-to-activation conversion;
- referred-customer subscription retention;
- Veterinary Summary generation rate;
- Veterinary Summary share/use rate;
- veterinarian usefulness score;
- time saved / time added;
- repeat recommendation rate;
- false-alert / misunderstanding / over-interpretation rate;
- D2C CAC from veterinary-prescriber channel.

A future group negotiation should be supported by measured adoption evidence rather than generic market enthusiasm.

Illustrative future evidence format:

> X independent clinics, Y attributable recommendations, Z% owner activation, A% six-month retention, B% practitioner-rated summary usefulness.

No number should be published until measured.

## 14. Role of the future veterinary sales representative

A delegate/representative should be added **after** the prescriber proposition is proven.

The role should be closer to **prescriber network development / technical ambassador** than classic box-selling.

Preferred opening message:

> **You do not need to stock or sell EMOPET. We want to show you the longitudinal summary that owners can bring back to consultation and see whether it is useful in your workflow.**

Primary representative KPI:

- **active prescribers recruited**, not clinic sell-in revenue.

Secondary KPIs:

- practitioner activation;
- recommendation frequency;
- referred-owner conversion;
- practitioner retention;
- training completion where applicable;
- quality of CRM feedback.

Only after these metrics work should resale/distribution KPIs be introduced.

## 15. Commercial sequencing

### Phase 0 — pre-incorporation / pre-proof

**Goal:** credibility and product usefulness, not sales volume.

Priority actions:

- scientific review and validation planning;
- Oniris / veterinary-science discussions;
- ENVT / other qualified veterinary reviewers where relevant;
- Serpell / C-BARQ scientific governance;
- VetTech ecosystem participation;
- build Veterinary Summary v0;
- recruit first 10–15 Founding Panel practitioners;
- conduct approximately 10 first interviews before broadening outreach.

**Do not hire a veterinary sales delegate at this stage.**

### Phase 1 — prototype / early veterinary proof

Expand only after the report format improves.

Suggested range: **20–50 early-adopter practitioners**.

Primary questions:

1. Did the summary help you understand the chronology faster?
2. Did it provide useful information that owner recall alone may have missed?
3. Did it save time or add time?
4. Was the separation between observation and interpretation clear?
5. Did any wording feel diagnostic or intrusive?
6. Which observations are actually useful in consultation?

### Phase 2 — D2C launch + prescriber network

D2C remains the main sales route.

Veterinary role:

- recommendation;
- early-adopter credibility;
- feedback;
- professional education;
- optional referral/Observation Pass without veterinarian commission, if legally cleared;
- owner-controlled report sharing.

### Phase 3 — structured veterinary distribution experiments

Only after product evidence and incorporation:

- test one independent multicard representative on a bounded territory;
- approach veterinary groups for structured pilots;
- evaluate selected purchasing/distribution structures;
- assess clinic resale only where economics make sense;
- approach insurers only under strict data-governance constraints.

Any independent representative mandate must receive legal review before signature, especially if the relationship could qualify as an agent-commercial arrangement with statutory termination consequences.

## 16. Veterinary group strategy

Do not approach major groups as the first validation route.

Preferred sequence:

**independent practitioners → measured prescriber evidence → limited group pilot → scale discussion**.

When approaching a group, propose a bounded pilot rather than broad national exclusivity.

Potential pilot design:

- 5–10 clinics initially;
- 8–12 week evaluation period;
- pre-defined success metrics;
- practitioner and ASV feedback;
- owner activation and retention measurement;
- explicit claim and data boundaries;
- no automatic national rollout.

Potential negotiation topics:

- pilot scope and duration;
- number of clinics;
- pre-agreed success criteria;
- staff training;
- Veterinary Summary workflow;
- pricing;
- optional resale economics;
- data controller/processor roles;
- publication / pilot evidence;
- co-marketing rights;
- name/logo usage;
- exclusivity.

**Exclusivity rule:** do not grant broad exclusivity without material reciprocal value, defined scope, duration and performance commitments.

## 17. Purchasing/distribution structures

Potential terms:

- catalogue/reference conditions;
- distributor margin;
- minimum order / stock;
- payment terms;
- returns;
- logistics;
- after-sales support;
- training material;
- territory and exclusivity.

These structures are potential later logistics/scale infrastructure. They should not define EMOPET's launch identity.

## 18. Independent multicard representative terms

Potential terms:

- territory;
- target accounts;
- prohibited competing portfolio;
- remuneration;
- expenses;
- technical-training obligations;
- CRM/reporting;
- duration;
- termination;
- ownership of commercial data and leads.

No representative agreement should be signed before qualified review of the legal status and termination economics.

## 19. Insurer channel — opportunity with a hard data wall

Animal-health insurers may be commercially interesting because they already have a direct owner relationship and an economic interest in prevention and retention.

Possible commercial models to explore later:

- subsidised hardware;
- member benefit;
- subscription contribution;
- co-branded prevention programme;
- genuinely anonymised/aggregated research or population-level insights where lawful and appropriate.

**Internal strategic guardrail:** insurer access to individual MAT/TAG/ELI behavioural or wellbeing data must not be assumed. In particular, EMOPET should not casually enable individual data to be used for underwriting, pricing, eligibility or claims decisions.

Any insurer-data model requires a dedicated legal, ethical and product-governance decision.

## 20. Claim language for the veterinary channel

Preferred vocabulary:

- longitudinal observation;
- individual reference / baseline;
- chronology;
- persistent change;
- contextual observation;
- level of confidence;
- information insufficient;
- owner-shared history;
- veterinary interpretation remains necessary.

Avoid without specific validation/legal basis:

- diagnosis;
- disease detection;
- pain detection;
- arthritis detection;
- clinical alert;
- medical monitoring;
- veterinary decision support;
- early diagnosis.

Even when a condition such as osteoarthritis is discussed internally as an example of why longitudinal observation may matter, EMOPET must not claim that MAT/TAG identifies that condition unless a separate validated and legally reviewed product claim is deliberately pursued.

## 21. Data-governance gate

The source research correctly identifies data sharing with veterinarians as a material governance issue. However, the precise lawful basis, controller/processor roles and contractual structure must be determined from the actual implementation.

**Do not encode the statement that “Article 32 GDPR requires explicit consent for every veterinary transfer” as legal doctrine.** Article 32 primarily concerns security of processing. EMOPET may still deliberately choose explicit owner-controlled sharing as a product/ethics rule, but legal basis and role allocation require qualified review.

Required before professional sharing goes live:

- data-flow map;
- controller/processor determination;
- lawful-basis assessment;
- owner-facing permission UX;
- revocation mechanism;
- access logging;
- retention rules;
- contractual framework where required;
- security review.

## 22. Go / no-go commercial doctrine

### GO

- D2C core launch;
- veterinarian recommendation;
- one-page Veterinary Summary;
- Veterinary Founding Panel;
- scientific/VetTech credibility strategy;
- warm-introduction acquisition;
- targeted Grand Ouest practitioner outreach;
- small early-adopter practitioner panel;
- measured referral attribution without veterinarian commission, subject to review;
- owner benefits for veterinarian-referred customers;
- later bounded group/distributor experiments.

### NOT YET

- nationwide delegate network;
- broad clinic stocking programme;
- exclusivity with a veterinary group;
- insurer access to individual EMOPET data;
- clinical/dashboard positioning;
- disease-specific marketing claims;
- large-scale cold outreach before the Veterinary Summary proposition has been tested.

### NO-GO WITHOUT A DELIBERATE PRODUCT RECLASSIFICATION / VALIDATION PROGRAMME

- presenting EMOPET as diagnosing a disease;
- claiming that a MAT/TAG pattern establishes pain, osteoarthritis, cardiac disease, anxiety disorder or another clinical condition;
- paying veterinarians in a manner tied to clinical decisions or hidden referral commissions;
- presenting scientific partners as endorsing EMOPET when they have not done so.

## 23. Near-term execution plan

### Immediate

1. Build **Veterinary Summary v0** using synthetic/example data only.
2. Build a one-page **Veterinary Founding Panel** invitation.
3. Define 8–10 structured interview questions.
4. Build the first **50-practice target list** in the Grand Ouest.
5. Request warm introductions through suitable existing scientific/regional contacts.
6. Recruit first **10 practitioners** for interviews.

### After first 10 interviews

1. Revise Veterinary Summary to v1.
2. Remove information practitioners consistently ignore.
3. Tighten wording where clinicians perceive diagnostic ambiguity.
4. Test owner-to-vet sharing journey.
5. Recruit next 10–20 practitioners.

### Before a major veterinary congress / VetTech event

1. Have a phone/tablet-ready Veterinary Summary prototype.
2. Prepare a 30-second explanation.
3. Prepare one QR interest form.
4. Capture practitioner role and clinic type.
5. Ask a short standard question set.
6. Separate veterinarian and ASV feedback.

### Before hiring a veterinary representative

Require evidence that:

- the Veterinary Summary is useful;
- practitioner positioning is understood;
- referral attribution works;
- referred owners convert at an acceptable rate;
- there is repeat recommendation behaviour;
- the commercial economics justify a field-acquisition cost.

## 24. Core commercial architecture

**D2C = economic engine**  
**Veterinarian = trust/prescription engine**  
**Science = legitimacy engine**  
**Veterinary Summary = product bridge**  
**Observation Pass / QR = attribution mechanism**  
**Founding Panel = product-learning engine**  
**Representative = later acquisition accelerator**  
**Veterinary groups = scale route**  
**Purchasing structures = optional logistics layer**

The central strategic principle is:

> **Do not pay someone to convince veterinarians before EMOPET has learned, directly from veterinarians, what is actually convincing and useful.**

## 25. Evidence status and caveats

This document is a strategic synthesis derived from the 2026-09-06 France veterinary-delegate/channel research, subsequent prescriber-strategy work and current EMOPET doctrine.

Important caveats retained from the source research:

- no official published count of French veterinary delegates / independents was identified;
- market consolidation figures can move quickly;
- distributor/clinic margin ranges are indicative and require real commercial quotations;
- current connected-device benchmarks do not prove EMOPET channel viability;
- group, resale, referral-code, representative and data-sharing structures require qualified legal/deontological review before execution;
- professional-network membership counts and event details must be re-verified before external publication or budgeting;
- none of the proposed veterinary-facing mechanisms constitutes scientific validation or veterinary endorsement.
