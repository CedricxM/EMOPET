# EMOPET — Veterinary Founding 12 & Prescriber Loop

**Date:** 2026-09-06  
**Status:** STRATEGIC WORKING DOCUMENT — NOT A CLINICAL VALIDATION CLAIM / NOT LEGAL ADVICE  
**Scope:** Early veterinary co-design, prescriber acquisition, observation-pass mechanics, Veterinary Summary testing, conference activation and scale sequencing.

## 1. Core idea

EMOPET should not begin by selling hardware into veterinary clinics. It should first recruit a small group of veterinarians and ASVs to help design the professional-facing information layer, then convert proven usefulness into a measurable prescriber network.

The core proposition is:

> **EMOPET does not tell the veterinarian what the dog has. It helps reconstruct what changed, when it changed, and how consistently it changed relative to that dog’s own baseline.**

Internal campaign territory:

> **Depuis quand ?**

This wording targets the consultation gap created by imprecise owner recall without claiming diagnosis.

## 2. EMOPET Founding 12 — Veterinary Continuity Program

Create a deliberately small founding panel of approximately 12 veterinarians plus a small number of ASVs.

Suggested composition:

- 4 general small-animal practitioners;
- 2 practitioners with a strong senior-dog caseload;
- 2 practitioners in internal medicine, pain, mobility or rehabilitation;
- 2 practitioners in behaviour;
- 2 innovation / VetTech-oriented practitioners;
- ideally 4 ASVs attached to selected participating clinics.

The panel is not an endorsement board and participants are not sales ambassadors by default.

### Initial commitment

Keep participation light:

- 2–3 interviews of approximately 30 minutes;
- review of simulated Veterinary Summaries;
- later prototype feedback where appropriate;
- optional participation in future structured pilots under separate documentation.

### Questions the panel should answer

1. Would you look at this information during a consultation?
2. Which observations are genuinely useful?
3. What is too detailed or too vague?
4. What language feels diagnostic or misleading?
5. What history window matters: 7, 30, 60, 90 days?
6. Does the summary save time or add work?
7. What would make you recommend EMOPET?
8. What would make you refuse to recommend it?
9. Should the default workflow be a one-page summary, a secure link, or a professional account?
10. What should never be surfaced to the owner or veterinarian without additional validation?

## 3. Veterinary Usefulness Index — internal product KPI

Create an internal, non-clinical usefulness score for product testing only.

Example 0–5 items:

- I would look at this report in consultation.
- This information would be difficult to reconstruct from owner recall alone.
- It helps me date the evolution.
- It saves rather than adds time.
- The separation between observation and interpretation is clear.

**Important:** this is a product-research KPI, not a validated scientific scale and must never be presented as clinical evidence.

Track raw item responses as well as the total so that weak areas are visible.

## 4. Veterinary Summary MVP

The preferred initial professional-facing object is a one-page Veterinary Summary rather than a permanent clinical dashboard.

Suggested fields:

- dog identity and basic context;
- observation period;
- baseline/reference period;
- persistent changes observed;
- approximate first persistent appearance;
- recurrence/frequency;
- confidence level;
- relevant context;
- periods of insufficient information / abstention;
- explicit boundary: EMOPET does not identify the cause or diagnose a condition.

The report should be readable in approximately 20 seconds.

## 5. Vet Link — low-friction owner-controlled sharing

Candidate experience:

1. Owner taps `Share with my veterinarian`.
2. EMOPET generates a secure time-limited link or report.
3. Veterinarian can read it without being forced into an account-creation workflow.
4. Owner remains in control of sharing.
5. Access is revocable/logged where technically applicable.

A permanent professional portal should only be built if field evidence shows it creates real value beyond the one-page summary / secure link.

## 6. Observation Pass

Create a clinic-specific referral mechanism called the **EMOPET Observation Pass** rather than a generic discount code.

Candidate mechanics:

- clinic-specific QR code;
- owner receives an EMOPET benefit;
- veterinarian receives no sales commission under the default candidate model;
- attribution is retained for measuring prescriber performance;
- no owner data is automatically shared with the clinic merely because the clinic originated the referral.

Potential owner benefits to test:

- 1–3 months of premium service;
- guided onboarding;
- Veterinary Summary included;
- extended trial/return period;
- priority support during baseline establishment.

Candidate disclosure:

> **Your veterinarian does not receive a sales commission for this recommendation.**

**Legal/deontological status:** model must be reviewed before launch.

## 7. Clinic-facing physical asset

Test a minimal physical card rather than a retail display.

Front:

> **Depuis quand ?**

Back concept:

> Some changes are easy to notice but difficult to date. EMOPET preserves a chronology of your dog’s routines so you can better describe what changed during a veterinary consultation. EMOPET does not diagnose disease.

Include the clinic Observation Pass QR code.

## 8. 30-Day Memory Test — exploratory research concept

Before claiming that owner memory is a commercially important pain point, test it.

Possible exploratory UX study:

- recruit volunteer owners;
- collect structured baseline descriptions;
- keep a lightweight diary for 30 days;
- later ask participants to date changes from memory;
- compare recall with their own contemporaneous records.

This may be useful as product research. It must not be presented as a clinical or scientific result without an appropriate protocol, ethics/governance review where needed, and sound methodology.

## 9. ASVs as internal champions

ASVs should be included early because they often shape practical clinic workflow and owner communication.

Candidate 20-minute ASV preview:

- what the Summary is;
- what it is not;
- how to receive/share/file it;
- how to explain it to an owner;
- when to route interpretation to the veterinarian;
- how to flag usability problems back to EMOPET.

Where possible, recruit one veterinarian + one ASV from selected Founding 12 clinics.

## 10. Recruitment strategy

### Priority 1 — warm introductions

Use existing scientific and ecosystem relationships to identify practitioners likely to give high-quality criticism.

Potential introduction routes:

- Oniris contacts;
- ENVT contacts;
- other scientific reviewers;
- AudéLor / regional ecosystem introductions;
- VetTech / professional networks.

The ask is not `find customers` but:

> **Could you introduce me to one or two small-animal practitioners who would be willing to critique whether this information would actually be useful in consultation?**

### Priority 2 — Grand Ouest targeted list

Build a first prospect list of no more than ~50 practitioners, biased toward:

- Lorient;
- Vannes;
- Nantes;
- Rennes;
- Angers and adjacent areas where useful.

Score by:

- small-animal/general-practice relevance;
- senior-dog caseload;
- longitudinal follow-up potential;
- pain/mobility/internal medicine/behaviour exposure;
- digital/VetTech openness;
- independence or influence inside the clinic;
- willingness to participate in product research.

### Priority 3 — independent veterinary networks

Use independent clinic networks as recruitment multipliers for the Founding Panel rather than asking for national product referencing too early.

Ask for access to a small number of innovation-oriented practitioners, not mass promotion.

## 11. Outreach message doctrine

Avoid generic startup outreach such as `AI-powered connected monitoring solution`.

Preferred framing:

> Bonjour Docteur,
>
> Je développe EMOPET, un projet français qui cherche à documenter les changements de routine d’un chien entre deux consultations, sans diagnostic ni interprétation clinique.
>
> Nous travaillons actuellement sur une synthèse d’une page destinée à répondre plus précisément à des questions simples : qu’est-ce qui a changé, depuis quand et avec quelle régularité ?
>
> Avant de la construire davantage, je cherche une dizaine de vétérinaires praticiens prêts à nous dire ce qui serait réellement utile ou inutile en consultation.
>
> Il ne s’agit ni d’une démarche commerciale ni d’un engagement à utiliser EMOPET. Je souhaiterais simplement vous montrer une maquette pendant 20 à 30 minutes et recueillir vos critiques.
>
> Seriez-vous disponible pour un court échange ?

## 12. Conference activation — `Depuis quand ?`

For AFVAC / VetTech environments, avoid a conventional IoT sales booth if possible.

Potential activation:

- headline: **DEPUIS QUAND ?**
- subline: `Votre client sait que quelque chose a changé. Mais sait-il quand ?`
- display one realistic Veterinary Summary;
- ask one question: `Si votre client vous apportait cela en consultation, est-ce que vous le regarderiez ?`
- capture structured feedback and contact permission.

If event-level response rates are displayed, label them clearly as event polling / product research, never as scientific evidence.

## 13. Future veterinary sales representative role

When economically justified, a veterinary representative should primarily recruit and activate prescribers rather than push clinic inventory.

Candidate representative KPI stack:

- new active veterinary prescribers;
- active prescribers retained at 30/90/180 days;
- Observation Pass scans;
- D2C conversion from vet-attributed referrals;
- Veterinary Summaries generated/opened;
- subscription retention of vet-referred clients;
- veterinarian usefulness score;
- complaint / over-interpretation rate.

Define:

**CPA-Vet = Cost per Active Veterinary Prescriber.**

Compare CPA-Vet and downstream value with D2C CAC before scaling the field-sales channel.

## 14. Scale flywheel

1. Scientific/ecosystem contact introduces practitioner.
2. Practitioner joins Founding 12 / product research.
3. Practitioner improves Veterinary Summary.
4. Clinic receives Observation Pass after the model is cleared.
5. Owner purchases D2C.
6. Owner establishes baseline and later shares a report.
7. Practitioner experiences the usefulness directly.
8. Practitioner recommends EMOPET more often where appropriate.
9. ASV integrates the workflow operationally.
10. Evidence of adoption supports structured discussions with veterinary groups.
11. Group pilots create scale.
12. Only then evaluate representatives, distribution and clinic resale more broadly.

## 16. Competitive / originality warning

This strategy is **not to be treated as a novel or protectable concept merely because it is new to EMOPET**.

Veterinary advisory boards, veterinary data-sharing portals, clinic-specific codes, professional reports and referral programs already exist in the pet-health market. Competitive research must therefore distinguish:

- common channel mechanics;
- EMOPET-specific execution;
- any genuinely differentiated technical or product IP.

The likely differentiation is not the abstract idea of `vet referral + data sharing`, but the combination of:

- individual longitudinal baseline;
- explicit confidence/abstention;
- observation/interpretation separation;
- low-friction consultation summary;
- non-commission prescriber positioning;
- owner-controlled data sharing;
- measured prescriber economics.

No originality, patentability or freedom-to-operate conclusion is implied by this document.
