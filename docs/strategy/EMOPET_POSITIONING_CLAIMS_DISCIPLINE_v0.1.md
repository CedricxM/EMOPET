## 1. Rule

EMOPET must not claim uniqueness from features already common or publicly disclosed in the category.

Do not build primary positioning around:
- personalized baselines;
- AI monitoring;
- change detection;
- early awareness;
- veterinary reports;
- veterinarian sharing;
- activity/sleep tracking;
- community maps/events;
- memory timelines;
- generic recommendations.

## 2. Candidate owned territory

EMOPET should test a positioning around **continuity + evidence discipline**, not feature count.

### Candidate message pillar A — continuity

> `EMOPET keeps the thread of everyday life with your dog.`

Product meaning:
- MAT + TAG provide complementary observation contexts;
- Care preserves qualified history;
- Memories preserve what the Guardian chooses to keep;
- Community/World connect local/shared life;
- professional sharing carries the relevant chronology forward.

### Candidate message pillar B — epistemic restraint

> `EMOPET is designed to know when the evidence is not good enough.`

Product meaning:
- provenance;
- quality/confidence;
- publish/degrade/reject;
- abstention;
- no forced emotional or medical certainty.

### Candidate message pillar C — no reduction to a score

> `A dog's everyday life is not one number.`

Product meaning:
- no generic health score;
- no relationship score;
- no good-Guardian score;
- no dog-performance social rank;
- observations remain contextual and longitudinal.

## 3. Veterinary positioning candidate

Do not sell `diagnosis` or `disease detection`.

Working professional territory:

> `Your client knows something changed. EMOPET helps preserve when, how often and with what observation confidence.`

This is a workflow/anamnesis hypothesis and must be tested with veterinarians before external use as a proven-benefit claim.

## 4. MAT positioning candidate

Until incremental-value evidence exists:

> `TAG follows the dog through the day. MAT gives EMOPET a dedicated resting observation context. EMOPET combines those contexts only when the evidence is good enough.`

Do not claim `more accurate`, `clinically superior`, `earlier detection` or equivalent without specific evidence.

## 5. Breiz positioning candidate

> `Breiz explains and contextualizes. It does not invent what the dog feels.`

Breiz must never be marketed as translating the dog's thoughts or emotions.

## 6. Community / World positioning candidate

> `Use EMOPET to find something worth doing, then put the phone away.`

Do not market engagement volume, streaks, virality or screen time as value.

## 7. Internal claims ladder

Use the weakest accurate statement supported by evidence:

1. `DESIGNED TO` — architecture/intention exists.
2. `IMPLEMENTED` — code/runtime evidence exists.
3. `TESTED` — bounded controlled test exists.
4. `VALIDATED FOR [specific condition]` — defined validation evidence exists.
5. `PROVEN TO [specific outcome]` — only when appropriate evidence directly supports the outcome.

Never jump levels because a competitor uses stronger language.

## 8. Forbidden internal shortcuts

Do not use without evidence:
- `bulletproof`;
- `clinically proven`;
- `market unique`;
- `first ever`;
- `more accurate than wearables`;
- `detects problems before symptoms`;
- `understands your dog` when it implies mental-state knowledge;
- `vet-approved` from informal scientific contact.

## 9. Strategic test

A candidate slogan/claim should survive this question:

> `If Tractive, Invoxia, PetPace or another competitor copied this sentence tomorrow, would EMOPET still have a deeper architecture and evidence contract behind it?`

If no, the sentence is a campaign line, not the moat.
