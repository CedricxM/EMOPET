# EMOPET — Supplier Chain Lineage — June–July 2026

**Audit date:** 2026-09-07  
**Status:** `RETROSPECTIVE CONTROLLED MEMORY / HISTORICAL WORKING STRATEGY`

## Purpose

Preserve the real evolution of the pre-MOKO supplier strategy so old supplier names are not mistaken for current selections.

## 1. Historical hybrid-chain concept

ClickUp records a working industrial principle:

> **Pas de fournisseur chef. Chaque fournisseur reste dans son couloir.**

The intended historical flow was broadly:

`component suppliers → harness/connectivity preparation → PCBA/integration → tested subassemblies → French ESAT final assembly/control/packaging`

This was a working strategy, not a fully qualified or contracted production chain.

## 2. Landztop / Nancy Chow

Gmail and ClickUp show that Landztop could fabricate/assemble according to EMOPET-provided documentation.

But a supplier message also stated that design documents were generally not provided back to clients.

ClickUp task `869dxt4nw` therefore classified Landztop as:

- possible PCBA fabrication candidate where the design is already owned by EMOPET;
- not appropriate for proprietary design responsibility unless source-file, IP, reuse, subcontracting and complete-deliverable protections are contractually secured;
- secondary / fabrication-only candidate at that historical checkpoint;
- not the main box-build or design authority.

This historical experience supports the current rule that supplier execution does not transfer EMOPET architecture authority.

## 3. Yz-Link / Katherine

ClickUp task `869dw7t3v` positioned Yz-Link as a potential supplier for:

- FFC/FPC;
- ZIF connectors;
- internal harnesses/connectivity;
- possible harness preparation/termination.

A possible logistics role was explored, but the file explicitly said Yz-Link was **not yet a validated hub**.

Open items included:

- legal/factory identity;
- actual factory vs trader status;
- custom pricing;
- receiving third-party components;
- handling/storage fees;
- coaxial-cable termination capability;
- continuity/flex tests;
- MOQ/lead time;
- no subcontracting without approval;
- NDA/NNN before sensitive files.

The approximately `$0.30` FFC/FPC figure was a generic listing, not an EMOPET quotation.

## 4. ESAT / France final assembly

The historical hybrid chain intended French final/manual assembly and control, with ESAT involvement as an operational/social-impact route.

This was explored across several Breton locations and suppliers. It was not a final production award.

## 5. July BOM reality check

ClickUp task `869dvjx9u` explicitly said the objective was to transform supplier conversations into a **real BOM + logistics matrix**, not keep adding components.

At that checkpoint:

- some sample/component prices were known;
- several important prices were still missing;
- PCBA, load cells, enclosures and logistics were insufficiently priced;
- hidden costs still needed to include shipping, battery transport, hub handling, customs/VAT, NRE, stencil, test fixture, programming, calibration, cable termination, scrap, packaging and ESAT operator/jig/control time.

The task concludes that total V1 cost was **not yet calculable**.

## 6. Transition to MOKO

The later MOKO workstream supersedes this chain as the active scoped engineering/RFQ route.

That does **not** mean the earlier work was useless. It established durable governance lessons:

- supplier roles must be scoped;
- sensitive disclosure must be limited;
- source/native deliverables matter;
- supplier qualification must include actual entity/site/subcontracting;
- quotation ≠ supplier selection;
- manufacturing capability ≠ design authority;
- logistics/hidden costs belong in the real BOM.
