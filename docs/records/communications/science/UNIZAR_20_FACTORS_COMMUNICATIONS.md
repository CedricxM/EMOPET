## Counterparties

EMOPET corresponded with Marta Siguín Calvo and co-authors of the 20-Factors Framework for wearable biosensing design.

This record must not be described as an institutional partnership, publication agreement or scientific validation unless later evidence establishes that status.

## 13 April 2026 — initial application of the framework

EMOPET presented the then-current dual-module system and said the 20-Factors Framework had become an important design reference.

The historical system described at that time included:

- stationary bed with PVDF piezoelectric array, load cells and environmental sensors;
- textile collar with IMU, microphone, piezo, thermistor and GPS;
- factor-by-factor mapping against all 20 factors.

EMOPET also said the framework had led to design changes around high-intensity canine-interaction context and the collar weight constraint.

**Historical status:** design application of an external framework, not validation of EMOPET.

## 16–17 April 2026 — review interest

The researchers replied that the application looked thoughtful and product-oriented, expressed interest in discussing EMOPET as a potential case study, and agreed to review technical documentation and the factor mapping.

EMOPET then sent/identified three main source families:

- ELI Scientific Foundation V6;
- Sensor System Specification V3;
- Modular Physical Architecture V1.

## 9 June 2026 — substantive external feedback

After reviewing the material, Marta Siguín Calvo said the documentation was detailed and gave a clear picture of the system logic.

The requested next evidence included:

1. the complete factor-by-factor table for all 20 factors;
2. a synthesis of the most relevant factor interrelationships;
3. clarification of project maturity, including whether a prototype or animal tests existed;
4. clarification of the collaboration sought.

Potential collaboration formats explicitly raised included:

- technical feedback on framework application;
- external review of the mapping;
- a more structured case study;
- possible academic publication.

The researchers also indicated that physical-integration details would benefit from additional photos, diagrams or assembly sketches.
